24 Hours on the Grid

Every day the United States rebuilds its electricity supply from scratch, hour by hour. This is the average day — how much power comes from each source at each hour, drawn from real 2024 readings of the U.S. grid. Pick a region and drag through the year to watch solar carve its midday valley into demand: the famous duck curve.

Source: U.S. Energy Information Administration, Hourly Electric Grid Monitor (EIA API v2), calendar year 2024. Each series is the mean generation for that hour-of-day across every day of the year, in the region's local time. "Other" includes petroleum and miscellaneous sources.

The city & utility grids are individual balancing authorities — the finest resolution at which hourly generation is published; nothing goes below it, because once power enters the grid it can't be traced to a particular neighborhood. Many of these small grids import a share of their power, so the mix shown is what's generated inside the territory; any gap up to the demand line is net imports from the wider grid.
